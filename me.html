My Tech Journey:

From a young age, I was fascinated by technology. It started with an old typewriter my grandfather used in his typewriting business — I was captivated by the machine, the keys, the act of turning thought into print. Growing up in a family of engineers — my father and two older siblings — immersed me in science and tech from the start. That environment shaped who I'd become and fueled a passion I've never lost.

Although technology was my primary focus, but never my only one. I sang and played guitar in a band, performing at events and competitions, and nurtured a love for poetry and songwriting. Photography became another outlet after a documentary about a National Geographic photographer caught my imagination, and I spent time on post-production editing for two short films during college. More recently, I've started exploring piano and tennis — proof that I'm still chasing new things.

At 12, I came across my sister's C and Java books, and that was the beginning of my journey into computers. The iPhone launch in 2007 only deepened the obsession. That same year, I wrote my first program — “Hello World” in Turbo C — and somehow knew, even then what I wanted to do for the rest of my life. I pursued a diploma after high school, followed by my undergraduate degree in computer science.

Beyond technology, I've always cared deeply about personal growth and giving back. I volunteered with the Art of Living Foundation, organizing yoga and meditation workshops across rural India. When the 2020 pandemic hit, I moved that work online — running wellness, leadership, and personality-development sessions for communities around the world. Few things have been as rewarding as watching that work land in someone else's life.

In my final year of undergrad, I competed in a hackathon with 100+ participants, which led to a round of interviews and my first internship at BMC Software. There I joined the team building BMC Helix Virtual Agent — an AI chatbot for BMC's internal digital workplace — during its pilot phase. I built a Java orchestration service that took employee requests understood through Google Dialogflow and routed them to the right system behind the scenes: booking time off through the HR tool, raising an IT ticket, even ordering a cab through a transport partner. It was my first real taste of shipping a product toward production, and it made me want to go deeper.

That pull led me to the US, where I earned my Master's in Computer Science at Indiana University Bloomington. Studying under exceptional professors and alongside genuinely sharp people, I felt myself getting ready for the next leap.

That leap was The Raley's Companies, where I started as an intern and grew into a full-time Software Engineer. As an intern, I built a full-stack in-store routing system for online order pickers — a pilot that used a modified Dijkstra's algorithm to find the shortest path through a store's aisles. That work turned into a full-time career building backend systems across two very different worlds: a brick-and-mortar grocery network — Raley's, NobHill, Belair, Basha's, AJ's, FoodCity, spanning 235+ stores and serving millions of customers — and a farm-to-door produce-subscription business, Farm Fresh To You and Fullcircle — that delivers the season's best straight to people's doors. Same engineer, two business models. The constant context-switching is one of the things I like most about the role.

Day to day, I work across the backbone of our eCommerce: the APIs behind product search and ordering, the Azure data pipelines feeding our loyalty and marketing platforms, the reporting infrastructure our finance and operations teams rely on, and the integrations tying payments, identity, and partners together. I'm a backend engineer at heart, but on a small team you wear a lot of hats — and I've come to love that range.
